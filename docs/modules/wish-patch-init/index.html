Overview

On a Hermetic Modular Alchemy Lab? Wish started there: Wish on Alchemy Lab.

Wish is a granular texture synthesiser for a stock Daisy patch.Init(), based on Mutable Instruments Beads — Émilie Gillet’s successor to Clouds. Mutable published the Beads source on 11 October 2026. The DSP here is that code, built exactly as Wish builds it for the Alchemy Lab. Same chip, same clock: the Patch Submodule is the same STM32H750 at 480 MHz. What changes is the panel: four knobs, one button, one toggle and one LED in place of Beads’ twelve knobs and four buttons.

Beads records whatever comes in, and plays it back as grains — short slices, each with its own pitch, length and envelope. FREEZE stops recording and holds the buffer. Grains come from the internal clock, or from triggers on the SEED jack. In Synth mode, chosen in SETTINGS, Beads plays grains from its own wavetables instead: FEEDBACK picks one of twelve banks, and TIME sweeps through it.

Named — like everything here — after the nursery rhyme: eight for a wish, the line the channel takes its name from. Not affiliated with, or endorsed by, Mutable Instruments or Electrosmith.

Controls

Four knobs, three layers. The toggle picks PLAY or TONE; holding B7 gives RANDOM. The four PLAY knobs are the four with jacks.

Layer Knob 1 Knob 2 Knob 3 Knob 4
PLAY — toggle down TIME SIZE DENSITY PITCH
TONE — toggle up SHAPE DRY/WET FEEDBACK REVERB
RANDOM — B7 held TIME random SIZE random SHAPE random PITCH random

A knob takes a parameter over only once it reaches the parameter’s value, so changing layer never makes anything jump.

Knob Job
TIME where in the buffer grains start; plus CV on CV_6
SIZE grain length: shortest at about half past ten; clockwise of that grains play forwards and grow to four seconds, anticlockwise they play backwards and grow too; plus CV on CV_7
DENSITY noon is no grains; anticlockwise regular, clockwise random; plus CV on CV_8
PITCH grain pitch, snapping through 0, 1, 3, 4, 7, 12 and 24 semitones; plus V/oct on CV_5
SHAPE grain envelope
DRY/WET the mix
FEEDBACK how much of the output goes back into the buffer
REVERB reverb amount
randomisers noon none; clockwise, every grain gets the same degree of randomness, set by the knob; anticlockwise the degree is itself random, so some grains stay put and others land anywhere. In delay mode, a smooth random wander
Button Job
B7 tap FREEZE, on and off. The LED is lit while frozen
B7 held the RANDOM layer, while held
B7 held 2 s turning nothing: SETTINGS. The LED breathes; tap B7 to leave
B7 at power-on V/oct calibration
Jack Job
CV_5 V/oct
CV_6 TIME CV

The LED is lit while frozen, and flashes on each trigger at gate in 1.

Settings

Hold B7 for two seconds without turning a knob. A knob changes its setting only once it is turned, and the LED blinks the new value’s number.

Knob Setting Anticlockwise to clockwise
Knob 1 Quality 1 digital · 2 vintage digital · 3 clean tape · 4 dirty tape
Knob 2 Input 1 mono (In L, twice the buffer) · 2 stereo · 3 synth (no input: Beads’ wavetables)
Knob 3 Gate out 1 off · 2 on: grain gates on gate out 1, and Beads’ mono mix on both audio outs

A new quality or input waits while frozen, as on Beads. The settings, the knobs of the two layers not under the hand, and the calibration are saved, and come back at power-on.

Making a capture to play

  1. Patch a sound into In L and play the phrase you want.
  2. Tap B7 at the moment you want to keep it. The buffer now holds the audio just before the tap.
  3. Play it as a texture: DENSITY clockwise of noon for a cloud, TIME to choose where in the capture, SIZE for grain length, PITCH to transpose.
  4. Or play it from a sequencer: gates into gate in 1, pitch into CV_5. Each trigger plays one grain at that pitch. DENSITY fully clockwise plays every trigger, noon none, and anticlockwise divides them.

Turn DRY/WET fully clockwise to hear only the capture, and tap B7 again to record a new one.

Delay mode

Turn SIZE fully clockwise and Beads stops making grains and becomes a delay. Clocked from triggers on gate in 1, it follows their tempo, and DENSITY picks the division. SIZE random (hold B7, knob 2) becomes the delay’s filter: anticlockwise low-pass, clockwise high-pass, noon none.

Calibration

Hold B7 through power-on. LED solid: patch 1 V into CV_5 and press B7. LED blinking: patch 3 V and press. A flurry means done. Until then it uses a sensible default.

Differences from Beads

  • Twelve knobs become four on three layers, and four buttons become one.
  • There is no SEED button. The internal clock runs, and DENSITY sets it. Triggers on gate in 1 take over, as on Beads, and the internal clock comes back a few seconds after they stop.
  • The input mode is a setting. Beads hears whether a cable is in its inputs; the patch.Init() cannot.
  • CV is always added at full depth, so the randomisers stay randomisers whether a jack is patched or not. What is lost is Beads’ patched-and-anticlockwise case, where CV sets the amount of randomness.
  • The grain gates have their own jack, so they no longer take audio R. As on Beads, the audio is mono while the gate output is on.
  • No SHAPE CV and no ASSIGN jack, and input gain is automatic only.
  • Dirty tape runs at 48 kHz. Beads slows its codec to 24 kHz for it; the Daisy’s codec has no such rate, so it takes Beads’ own 48 kHz path.
  • The heaviest settings thin themselves. Digital stereo with dense grains and random pitch asks for more than the chip has, so past that point fewer new grains start, and the module stays responsive.
  • A frozen buffer is not kept through a power cycle.

Provenance

The DSP is Mutable Instruments Beads by Émilie Gillet, MIT-licensed, from pichenettes/eurorack. Built on Electrosmith’s libDaisy. Wish on Daisy patch.Init() itself is MIT. Thanks to Émilie for opening the code up.

Flash it — no compiler needed

Wish runs from the Daisy's SRAM through the Daisy bootloader: a one-time bootloader install, then the firmware goes on with an SD card. No compiler needed, and no hardware changes — it runs on a stock patch.Init().

Read this first — The card must be one FAT32 partition of 2 GB or less, with the rest of the card left unallocated. A full-size FAT32 volume on a big card fails at start-up, and exFAT is not read at all.
Download firmware v1.0.0Daisy Web Programmer

One time per module — install the bootloader

  1. Plug the Daisy Patch.Init in with a USB-C data cable.
  2. Hold BOOT, tap RESET, then release BOOT.
  3. Open the Daisy Web Programmer and go to its Bootloader section.
  4. Click Flash. That is the bootloader on — you never have to do this again.

Every time — load the firmware

  1. Format the card: one FAT32 partition of 2 GB or less. On a Mac, check the disk number with diskutil list external first — this erases it — then diskutil partitionDisk /dev/diskN MBR "MS-DOS FAT32" DAISY 2G "Free Space" REST R.
  2. Copy the .bin above to the root of the card. It must be the only .bin on the card.
  3. Insert the card and power-cycle. The bootloader flashes the firmware if it differs from what is installed, and boots. The LED stays dark until you freeze.
  4. Take the card out. Wish doesn't use it.
On a Mac, Finder writes a hidden twin of the file — ._wish_patch_init-v1.0.0.bin — and it confuses the bootloader. After copying, delete it in Terminal with rm -f /Volumes/DAISY/._*. Building from source? make program-dfu flashes over USB during the bootloader's two-second window after a reset.
