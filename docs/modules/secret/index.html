Overview

Secret is a chaotic oscillator. It runs strange attractors — sets of equations that never quite repeat — fast enough to hear, and plays them from a V/oct input like any other voice.

The problem with a chaotic oscillator is the word oscillator. Left alone, most of these systems have no steady pitch. Secret runs each one at a speed set by V/oct, so it plays near the note from the start, and adds one control, TAME. At zero the attractor runs free. Turn it up and the module steers it onto one of its own orbits that repeats at the note — keeping its grit at first, then settling into a clean, periodic tone. The interesting part is the middle.

It runs on the Hermetic Modular Alchemy Lab, an open DSP platform with a Daisy inside, six knobs with LED rings, three buttons and six CV jacks.

Named — like everything here — after the nursery rhyme: seven for a secret, never to be told. A system that is completely determined, and still can’t be predicted. Not affiliated with, or endorsed by, Hermetic Modular.

Tame — from noise to a note

TAME is delayed feedback control, from the chaos-control literature (Kęstutis Pyragas, 1992). Secret compares the voice with itself one note-period ago, and nudges one of its equations towards repeating. Once the system is on an orbit of its own that repeats at the note, the nudge falls to nothing — so the tone TAME settles on is the attractor’s own, not a waveform laid over it.

How much that does depends on the system:

  • Spirals (Rössler, Coupled Rössler, Moore–Spiegel) settle most easily: a small nudge finds their orbit.
  • Driven systems (Duffing, the pendulum) have their subharmonics drawn onto the note. Hindmarsh–Rose’s bursts become regular.
  • Van der Pol and the Brusselator are periodic already, so TAME has little to do.
  • Lorenz, Chua and Lorenz–Lü–Chen have no orbit it can find, so TAME leaves them wild. That is their nature.

Twelve models

B1 steps through all twelve. Both its LEDs show the current model’s colour.

The first six came from the Teensy. The second six were picked by ear from nine measured candidates — and none of them, as far as I could find, has been played as a V/oct voice in Eurorack hardware before.

Seeing it

Patch J7/J8 into an X/Y scope and you see the shape the sound is making: Rössler’s spiral, Lorenz’s butterfly, Chua’s double scroll. It is also the best tuning aid there is — a locked note draws a figure that stands still, and a slipping one turns.

Known limits

  • Four models top out inside the playing range at mid settings — Hindmarsh–Rose around 190 Hz, Chua 520, Colpitts 660 and Moore–Spiegel 880. Above that the note stops rising rather than the module overloading.
  • On Colpitts, the orbit TAME finds sits about three semitones from the free voice.
  • V/oct uses the board’s own calibration, which sets each jack’s zero but not its scale.
  • CV is read at 1 kHz, so there is no audio-rate FM.
  • No presets. The button choices reset at power-on.

Provenance

Joranalogue’s Orbit 3 is an analogue double scroll with V/oct and a tame/wild switch, and there is a long line of attractors as slow CV. TAME is Kęstutis Pyragas’s delayed feedback control, with the delay set by the note.

Built on Hermetic Modular’s MIT-licensed Alchemy SDK and Electrosmith’s libDaisy. Secret itself is MIT. Thanks to Luke Pendergrass at Hermetic Modular for the Alchemy SDK.

Flash it — no compiler needed

The Alchemy Lab ships with Hermetic Modular's own bootloader, so there is nothing to install first and no SD card involved. The firmware goes on over the front-panel USB-C from a web page. No compiler needed.

Read this first — Not the Daisy route. There is a Daisy Seed inside, but do not use the Daisy Web Programmer or a .bin on an SD card — the Alchemy Lab has its own bootloader, and its own programmer.
